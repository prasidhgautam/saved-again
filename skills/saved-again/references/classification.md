# Classification guide

Use this guide when assigning, auditing or revising categories. The goal is a useful personal filing system, not a maximal list of keyword matches.

## Evidence order

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. A manual category correction or an explicit category the user confirmed.
2. The post's inspected subject and practical purpose, using its owner caption, inspected slides or visible frames.
3. Speech or on-screen text that was actually extracted from legitimately available media.
4. A meaningful Instagram collection name.
5. Hashtags, creator names and generic thumbnail context.

Treat engagement prompts, boilerplate, unrelated examples and incidental tool names as weak evidence. Never turn a failed inspection or inaccessible reel into inferred content.

## Choose the primary category

Ask: “Why would the user reopen this save?” File it by that main use, not by the first noun in the caption.

- **AI & Filmmaking:** making, writing, shooting, editing, generating or producing film/video/audio/image work.
- **Marketing:** advertising, audience research, campaigns, copy, brand strategy, distribution, SEO or growth.
- **Food:** a dish, cooking method, recipe or meal idea. A metaphor such as “a recipe for better characters” is not Food.
- **AI & Technology:** software, coding, automation or an AI tool as the main subject. An AI tool used mainly to generate video belongs in AI & Filmmaking.
- **Places & Experiences:** travel plans, destinations, restaurants, cafés or activities at a place.
- **Learning & Ideas:** concepts, books, history, education, business principles or general reference material.
- **Fitness & Wellbeing:** exercise, movement, recovery, mental health or a wellbeing routine.
- **Style & Everyday:** clothing, styling, home techniques or practical everyday ideas.
- **Cinema & Culture:** work to watch, hear or appreciate—films, recommendations, music, poetry, humour, games or cultural references. A filmmaking tutorial is not Cinema & Culture.
- **Career & Work:** job search, résumés, interviews, employment rights or workplace decisions.
- **Needs review:** evidence is missing, low-confidence, conflicting or too generic to support a useful filing decision.

Choose one primary category. Add a secondary membership only when the save has a separate, concrete use in that category; do not cross-file merely because a broad word such as “AI,” “content,” “film,” “job” or “food” appears.

## Confidence gate

- **High:** the purpose is explicit and at least one strong source supports it.
- **Medium:** the likely purpose is useful but relies on partial evidence. Keep the classification reviewable.
- **Low:** the category depends on a broad keyword, generic caption or unresolved conflict. Use Needs review.

Never promote confidence because several weak signals repeat the same ambiguous wording. Preserve manual corrections on later imports.

## Quality check

For a material import or classifier change, review synthetic boundary cases plus a held-out sample of user-confirmed corrections. Inspect all low-confidence results and a sample from every category. Report accuracy only against a labelled evaluation set; raw category agreement is not ground truth. Repeated corrections should become reusable examples or scoring signals, not post-specific hard-coded exceptions.
